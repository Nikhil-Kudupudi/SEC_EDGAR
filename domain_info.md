# Breakdown of the Taxonomies

- **dei (Document and Entity Information)**: Contains metadata about the filing and the company itself. It is required on the cover page of almost every SEC filing. It includes facts such as the company's legal name, Central Index Key (CIK), trading symbol, fiscal year-end, and common shares outstanding.

- **us-gaap (U.S. Generally Accepted Accounting Principles)**: The massive, core financial taxonomy maintained by the Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB). It contains tens of thousands of tags representing line items on standard financial statements, such as Revenues, Net Income, Assets, and Operating Cash Flows.

- **srt (SEC Reporting Taxonomy)**: Also maintained by the FASB, this taxonomy is designed to bridge the gap between financial statements and specific SEC-mandated disclosure rules. It includes elements for required financial schedules, oil and gas disclosures, and specialized dimensional metrics used alongside GAAP data.

- **ecd (Executive Compensation Disclosure)**: This taxonomy is used to track executive pay metrics. It became widely prominent following the SEC's "Pay Versus Performance" rules, forcing companies to digitally tag executive compensation data tables, peer group performance, and alignment metrics.

- **ffd (Filing Fee Disclosure)**: A newer taxonomy implemented by the SEC to standardize how registration and transaction fees are tagged. It allows companies to structure their fee-related tables in Inline XBRL format to automatically validate that they have paid the correct regulatory filing fees.

- **invest (Investment Taxonomy)**: A specialized SEC-supported taxonomy used primarily by investment companies, funds, and financial institutions to detail schedules of investments, security holdings, and related financial instruments.
